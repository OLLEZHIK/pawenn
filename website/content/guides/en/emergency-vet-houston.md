## Your pet had an encounter with a bat or wild animal

In Texas and Harris County, bats, skunks, raccoons, coyotes, and foxes are high-risk carriers of rabies. If your pet has physical contact with any wild mammal or a bat, confine the pet safely without touching the wild animal and contact animal authorities immediately.

Under Texas Department of State Health Services protocols, vaccinated pets must receive a veterinary rabies booster and complete an observation period, whereas unvaccinated animals face extended isolation or euthanasia. If a bat is found inside your home or near sleeping family members, do not touch or release it; call 311 or animal control so the bat can be collected and tested.

## Your dog bit someone

Under the Texas Rabies Control Act (Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 826) and Chapter 6 of the Houston Code of Ordinances, any animal bite or scratch that breaks human skin must be reported promptly to authorities by dialing 311 or calling the BARC quarantine office at 832-395-9049.

A domestic dog that bites a person must undergo a mandatory **10-day observation quarantine**, regardless of vaccination history. The quarantine monitors the dog for clinical signs of rabies shedding. Depending on local rabies authority approval, quarantine may take place at an approved veterinary hospital, animal shelter, or at the owner's home under strict containment conditions. All boarding, impoundment, and veterinary costs during quarantine are the legal responsibility of the pet owner.
